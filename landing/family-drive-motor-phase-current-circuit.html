Drive Motor Phase Current Circuit

Diagnostic & Technical Explanation
Code P0A51 sets when the drive motor inverter's phase-current sensors report one of the three motor phases missing, out of balance with the other two, or exceeding the commanded value for the present torque request. Because the drive motor carries the highest current of any motor in the system, the most common physical causes are a corroded or loose high-current phase-lead connection at the inverter's output terminals, a damaged Hall-effect or shunt phase-current sensor, and a shorted or open turn within the motor's stator winding itself. A single-phase imbalance under load, rather than a total loss of all three phases, points toward a winding or connection fault on that one phase specifically.

These 86 diagnostic trouble codes share one detection mechanism and one repair path, which is why MechanicDB groups them into a single authored fault family rather than repeating the same explanation 86 times.
