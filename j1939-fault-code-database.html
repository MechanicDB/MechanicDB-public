Questions

Is this the SAE J1939 Digital Annex?

No. MechanicDB Heavy Duty does not reproduce the SAE J1939 Digital Annex, any J1939 DBC file, or SPN/FMI text licensed from SAE. Every fault pair is compiled from a published OEM fault-code document — a service manual, maintenance guide or DTC list hosted by the manufacturer itself — with our own wording and a per-row link to the document it came from.

Which heavy-duty systems are covered?

Transmission, ABS/ESC, engine ECU, aftertreatment and body controllers across 11 OEMs: Bendix, Caterpillar, Cummins, Deutz, Eaton, John Deere, Navistar, Perkins, PSI, WABCO, Yanmar — off-highway, stationary, marine, agricultural and specialty applications. On-highway engine fault lists from Cummins, Detroit Diesel, PACCAR, Volvo/Mack and Caterpillar trucks are dealer-portal material and are not included.

What are SPN and FMI in a J1939 fault code?

A J1939 fault is a pair. The SPN (Suspect Parameter Number) names the parameter or component a controller is reporting on, such as coolant temperature or a wheel-speed sensor. The FMI (Failure Mode Identifier, 0 to 31) says how it failed: above or below its range, shorted high or low, open, erratic, not updating and so on. Standard SPNs mean the same thing on every make; SPNs from 516096 up are assigned by each manufacturer, which is why the dataset names those per OEM.
