OBD-II fault codes in numbers

Aggregate statistics computed from the full MechanicDB dataset: 15,886 diagnostic trouble codes (9,249 SAE-standard, 6,637 manufacturer-specific across 32 makes) mapped to 56,561 probability-ranked repair procedures with difficulty, parts-cost and labor estimates. These figures describe the catalogue, not how often codes are set on the road. Every figure states its denominator and is free to cite, quote and embed with a link to this page.

Fault codes by vehicle system

88.1% of the 15,886 catalogued codes are powertrain codes (P-codes): 7,354 SAE-standard and 6,637 manufacturer-specific. Network (U) holds 1,228, Chassis (C) 478 and Body (B) 189. All 6,637 manufacturer-specific codes in the catalogue are powertrain codes.

Which makes have the most manufacturer-specific codes

Volkswagen has the most manufacturer-specific codes in the catalogue with 528 (8.0% of 6,637), followed by Ford, Lincoln and Mercury with 413 each. The 32 makes range from 528 down to 2 codes (Audi).

Code strings that mean different things per make

P1121 and P1690 are each defined by 21 of the 32 makes, with 9 and 7 distinct meanings respectively. 745 of the 798 manufacturer-specific code strings (93.4%) are defined by more than one make, and none of them reuses an SAE-standard string.

Which fault families hold the most codes

ignition coil control circuit is the largest of the 647 fault families with 397 codes (2.5%), followed by throttle position sensor circuit (334) and fuel injector circuit (286). Every code carries 3 to 5 ranked fixes (7,365 codes with 3, 8,139 codes with 4, 382 codes with 5); the most frequent most-probable first step, Inspect and reseat the ignition coil connector, is ranked first for 397 codes.

How much of the repair work is DIY

Across all 56,561 ranked fixes, 26.6% are Easy DIY, 39.3% Moderate DIY and 34.1% Professional Required. Looking only at the most probable cause of each code, 80.5% of the 15,886 rank-1 fixes are Easy DIY and 3.8% need a professional. Body codes have the highest professional share among rank-1 fixes; see the table.

What the repairs are estimated to cost

Every one of the 56,561 fixes carries a parts-cost range (100.0% coverage). The median range is $15 to $60 in parts; for the most probable fix per code it is $8 to $25. 37.8% of fixes top out under $50 and 2.2% (1,228) at $1,000 or more; the highest upper estimate is $5,862 (Replace the HV battery pack (or the worst-performing modules)).

How long the repairs are estimated to take

The median fix is estimated at 0.8 h of labor; 29.2% of fixes are 0.5 h or less and 1.1% are over 4 h, with a maximum of 10 h. By difficulty the medians are 0.4 h (Easy DIY), 0.8 h (Moderate DIY) and 1.5 h (Professional Required).

Which parts and supplies the fixes call for

The 56,561 fixes map to 75,055 replacement-part entries, 1.33 per fix on average (68.0% with 1, 31.4% with 2, 0.6% with 3), drawn from 1,250 distinct catalogue items. Automotive wiring repair kit (solder + heat-shrink) is mapped to the most fixes (8,090, 14.3%), ahead of Electrical contact cleaner (6,626) and Digital multimeter (3,460).

Method, reuse and citation

  • Source. The full MechanicDB build of 2026-07-13: 15,886 code definitions, 56,561 ranked fixes and 75,055 part mappings in 647 hand-authored fault families. Code definitions originate in SAE J2012 / ISO 15031-6 via an MIT-licensed upstream compilation; the repair content is original editorial material from a deterministic, test-gated pipeline. See Sources and the data dictionary.
  • Catalogue, not incidence. MechanicDB records what each code means and how it is repaired. No figure on this page measures how often a code appears on real vehicles; "most common" here always means most common within the catalogue, and each section says which denominator it uses.
  • Estimates are estimates. Parts costs (USD) and labor hours are the dataset's editorial values at build time, useful for comparison between fixes, not quotes. Difficulty is an editorial tier. Repair steps are educational reference material, not professional repair advice; high-voltage and SRS/airbag work requires qualified technicians.
  • Refresh. MechanicDB is curated; this page and its charts are regenerated with each new build, so figures move. Cite the build date.
  • Reuse. The figures and charts on this page are published under CC BY 4.0: use them in forum posts, articles, slides and videos with a link to https://mechanicdb.dataengineered.io/stats/. The machine-readable version is data.json. The row-level dataset is a separate commercial product; a free 90-code sample is in the public repository.
